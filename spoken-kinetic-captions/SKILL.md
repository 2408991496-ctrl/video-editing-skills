# Spoken Kinetic Captions

Use this skill when the user asks for animated emphasis text, text above the speaker, or words that pop in one by one during a talking-head video.

## Inputs
- The current talking-head footage and an editable transcript or word-level speech timings.
- The exact phrases to emphasize and any visual references. If none are specified, select only a few important phrases from the spoken content.
- The video aspect ratio, safe areas, the speaker's head position, and the project's existing typography and colors.

## Workflow
1. Check the spoken audio and transcript before setting timing. Preserve the actual wording. Reveal English text one word at a time; reveal Chinese text character by character or in short semantic groups, unless the user specifies otherwise.
2. Align each word's entrance to when it is spoken. Build the phrase progressively instead of showing the full sentence at its first word. Hold the completed phrase briefly after the last word.
3. Place emphasis text above the speaker whenever possible. Match the project's bold type, colors, outlines, and restrained pop animation. Break long phrases into readable lines without covering the face or ordinary subtitles.
4. If a frame will serve as the video cover, make sure that frame shows the complete phrase and that every character remains inside the canvas.
5. Use emphasis text selectively. Keep ordinary subtitles for the full speech so the top of the frame does not stay crowded.

## Quality checks
Inspect the first, middle, and last word of every emphasized phrase, plus the final hold frame. Confirm that the reveal follows the audio, the complete phrase is visible at the end, and nothing is clipped or overlaps the speaker's face or lower subtitles. If word timings are unavailable, obtain or correct them before animating.
